### I raised a crook?!

The Milwaukee Tribune
Milwaukee bank, police nap suspect passing phony checks
By Griffin Sanders

MILWAUKEE - Bank employees at the Milwaukee branch of First National Bank on Third Street helped police catch a young man who tried to counterfeit checks in their bank Sept. 6 at 2:30 p.m.

The man came into the bank with phony checks and identification that matched everything on the checks, Milwaukee Police Sgt. Fred Liotta said. Bank employees noted the license plate and a description of the car, called the police, and identified the young man after the Milwaukee Police stopped the car.

The suspect has been identified as Dexter Egan, 18, of Butter Ridge, the adopted son of the prominent chocolate milk tycoon, Ezra Wickford.

"He had three different sets of false ID matching the checks," Liotta said. "At 18, the kid is already like a pro."

Egan was arrested on felony charges of possession of false ID for purposes of fraud, possession of counterfeit checks, attempting to pass the checks, and conspiracy. If convicted, he could face a prison sentence of up to 20 years.

As of last Friday, Egan was being held at the County Correctional Center on $50,000 bail, said a jail spokeswoman.

Ezra Wickford could not be reached for comment, nor has anyone come forward to post bail for Mr. Egan.

### I'd rather see it burn

Rockford, Belcher, and Blech
Attorneys at law
17 Franklin Avenue, Suite 101
Madison, Wisconsin

June 3, 1949

Dear Ezra:

I have changed your will according the directions you gave me in our meeting of May 21. In the event of your death, Dexter will have no recourse to inherit Wickford Castle or any of your estate or financial holdings. Further, he will be unable to claim any association with or capitalize upon your name or reputation. I am currently proceeding with your request to annul your adoptive relationship to Dexter, and to sever any subsidiary legal ties you may have to him. I will notify when these procedures are finished.

On a personal note, allow me to say how sorry I am to hear of Dexter's criminal conviction and to urge you not the blame yourself. I'm sure you did the best you could with Dexter, but some children just turn out to be bad eggs. Nonetheless, while your disappointment must be profound, I commend your prudence and pragmatism in the decisions you have made to protect your estate.

Yours Faithfully,
*Peter Rockford*
Peter Rockford
