My first game was Nancy Drew: Haunted Carousel. I remember being about to use the audio cues to work out the harmonica puzzle and expected to play much more if necessary to Miles the Magnificent Memory Machine. When I was around that age, I was reading the Nancy Drew Notebooks, the younger geared Nancy and friends where she had a chocolate lab named Chip. I totally copied her when naming my own plush pup. All this to say, Nancy Drew has been a big part of my life for quite a while. 

That isn't to say other mysteries and detectives weren't an influence on me. It has always been one of my favorite genres. I was distraught when Steve went to college in Blues Clues and noticed the differently colored barrel that was about to move in Scooby Doo. I devoured the Encyclopedia Brown, Cam Jenson, and Boxcar Children stories, insisting I read them in order until they were checked out at the library. My family has many variations of the game 'Clue' (the DVD version still having some ear worm quotes). As escape rooms became popular, I felt like my own knowledge and practice in various games made me pretty prepared as I've done over a dozen ones so far. 

What do I do? I did a little coding in Nancy Drew and now I do a lot of coding as my full time job. Still is looking a lot at documentation. It's very Deadly Device, except only on the computer. You know that person behind a desk all the time with a monitor? That's me, except I have more than one monitor. I don't consider my job as particularly flashy, but it pays well and allows me time to work on personal projects after work.

I've been playing more mystery games than reading them these days. But I'm learning to be okay with that. And also recommend so many of them, I really love them and want to get perfection on every game I play it's a problem. I probably will make a list eventually, but that's another side quest in this massive project.  I took a game design class in college (it was bad, too much randomness), but it did give me an appreciation and desire to examine triggers and how different aspects come together. This project has shown me a lot about game design and how different aspects succeed or fall short.
