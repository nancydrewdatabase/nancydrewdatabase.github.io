### Stuff to Remember

DOTS, BARS AND GODS:
Sonny's I'd rather be counting sheep Guide to the MAYA NUMBER SYSTEM:

Zero looks like a turtle's shell but no turtle
1 dot = one; 2 dots = two, etc.
1 bar = five (so two bars = 2 x 5, or 10)

picture this: each dot is an individual finger print (greasy from spicy wantons!)...and the bar represents the whole hand, like a set of five fingers.

A bar with a dot next to it = 5 + 1 = 6 ... so 3 bars and a dot = 16, yes?

- Bars are usually vertical (up and down)
- Dots should be to the left of the bars.

Here's the catch: if it doesn't look exactly like a dot or bar, it doesn't count. Squigglies, ovals, incomplete dots, crooked bars, chocolate bars, mini-stars, horseshoes, shamrocks, diamonds, half-moons, scribbles, dribbles... none of these count! Also the Maya often put decorative marks next to their numbers so just ignore anything that's NOT a bar and/or dot.

I repeat, anything that doesn't look like a plain old dot or bar is out!

Easy as sleeping on the job, right? Not quite!

Some gods also represent numbers. They're pretty tough to pick out at first, but they'll grow on you--just like mold on cheese.

Some good rules of thumb:

- ONE (hun) has fish lips, a really big nose and a set of good teeth
- TWO (kaa) has a big hat with five 'fingers' and a semi-oval in it
- THREE (ox) looks like a doctor
- FOUR (kan) has his (or her?) tongue sticking out
- FIVE (ho) has wrinkles and a fancy hat
- SIX (wahk) wears an eye patch with an X in it.
- SEVEN (wuc) wears rectantular glasses and a small oval on the forehead
- EIGHT (waxac) has a curly forehead ornament and a peace symbol on cheek
- NINE (bolon) has a beard or acne
- TEN (lahun) looks like death - no nose and 'removable' jaw
- ELEVEN (buluk) is frothing at the mouth and has three curly ovals on the head
- TWELVE (lahka) ???
- THIRTEEN (oxlahumn) has a T on the cheek
- FOURNEEN (kanlahun), FIFTEEN (hulahun), SIXTEEN (waklahun), SEVENTEEN (wuklahun), EIGHTEEN (waxaklahun), and NINETEEN (bololahun) all look like the original numbers put with the 'removable' jaw of 'lahun'.
