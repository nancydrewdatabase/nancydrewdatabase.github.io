## Text

### Rutherford Slab (Exhibit J)
Palenque, Chipas, Mexico
Late Classic Period

The famous British explorer, Archibald Rutherford, discovered this piece in 1884 within the Palenque temple. The text on the slab commemorates Pacal's ascension 'tzolkin' date of 6 Lamat.

On load from the Museo de Antigual, Guatemala

### Marriage Lintel
Yaxhilan, Chiapas, Mexico
Late Classical Period

The marriage between Shield Jaguar and Lady Xoc is represented in this piece. Lady Xoc offers a shield and jaguar helment to her betrothed.

Beech Hill Permanent Collection

### Bird Jaguar & Captive (Exhibit H)
Yaxchilan, Chiapas, Mexico
Late Classic Period, CA. 755-770 CE

This panel is one of several commissioned by Bird Jaguar and details his capture of a *cahal*, or prominent local noble man.

Beech Hill Permanent Collection

### Lady Xoc Vision Lintel M
Yaxchilan, Chiapas, Mexico
Late Classic Period, 725 CE

This lintel shows Lady Xoc, Shield Jaguar's wife, celebrating her husband's accession, 9.12.9.8.1 5 *lmix* 5 Mac (October 23, 681 CE), Lady Xoc kneels with her *huipil* tied around her knees. The *huipil* is a traditional woven Maya blouse that holds both symbolic and ceremonial significance. The Maya believed that *huipils* had magical powers.

On loan from the [[content/04 TRT/Environments|Wickford]] Estate

### Sculpture (throneback?)
Usumacinta Valley, Mexico
Classic Period

The seated figures are a royal couple whose detail is unusual for this area. The function of the artifact is unknown but may have been part of a throne. Inscriptions reveal that the piece was dedicated to the goddess of the moon, Ixchel.

On load from the Aracari Foundation

### Stella of Noble
Calakmul, Campeche, Mexico
Late Classic Period

Little is known about this relief. It depicts a noble standing on a captive. Note the elaborate headdress and cape containing references to Tlaloc, the Rain God.

Beech Hill Permanent Collection

### Bicephalic Alter (Exhibit C)
Acropolis, Copan, Honduras
Late Classic Period, CA. 800 CE

This alter was dedicated during the reign of the sixteenth ruler of Copan, Yax Pasah. The focal point of this artifact is the large two-headed crocodile. This creature is called the "Bicephalic Monster," and it represents the continuous cycle of life and death.

Beech Hill Permanent Collection
